Identify the geography of this story from what the story SAYS, not from what
you know about the publication carrying it.

Report a place when the story's own reporting gets you there — either because
the article names the place, or because the article names something that IS in
that place: a school, a college, a hospital, a courthouse, a county office, a
business, a stadium, a team, an agency. Naming Tolton Catholic High School
locates a story in Columbia. Naming Westminster College locates one in Fulton.
Naming the Jackson County Detention Center locates one in Jackson County. That
is reporting, and you should use it.

For every place you report, quote the phrase from the article that got you
there — the place name itself, or the institution you reasoned from.

What you must NOT do is start that chain outside the story. None of these is
reporting, and none of them may produce a place on its own:

- The dateline. "COLUMBIA, Mo. (KMIZ)" is the newsroom's address, not the
  story's subject. A dateline is usable ONLY when it names somewhere other
  than the outlet's own city.
- The publication's identity — its name, masthead, domain, or the market you
  know it serves. A local outlet is usually covering its own community, and
  where the article names that community, or names something in it, report it
  normally. What you must not do is supply the outlet's city for a story that
  gives you no other reason to name it.
- A reporter's or contributor's biography, sign-off, or contact line.
- A person's biography — where someone was born, studied, or used to live.
  "Faith grew up in South Carolina before moving to Nashville to attend
  Belmont University" does not make this a Nashville story, and a candidate's
  degree from the University of Missouri does not make one a Columbia story.
- Lists of other headlines: "Popular Stories", "Most Read", "More from",
  "Trending", related-story rails.
- Weather widgets and forecasts, navigation, menus, subscription and consent
  notices, advertisements.

Then, from the places you have justified: name the CENTRAL geography — the one
place where the events, people or institutions at the center of the story are
located — and list the rest as mentions.

Where the story is centred on a county or a region rather than a single town,
give the county and leave the city empty. Do not reach for the county seat or
the largest city to fill the gap: a story about the Jackson County Detention
Center is a Jackson County story, not a Kansas City story, unless the article
gives you Kansas City.
